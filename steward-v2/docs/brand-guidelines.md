# Steward — Design Brief and Brand Guidelines

Oct 17, 2026 · @nikolaj

## Design brief

Design Steward as a premium, durable and slightly playful tool: stainless steel and aluminium, quietly confident, never fussy.

| Item | Brief |
| --- | --- |
| **Product** | Steward, the AI office manager (web app and Slack), and the Steward Bot, a handheld multi-tool with a camera, screen, voice and six folding tools |
| **Audience** | Office managers first; also budget holders, suppliers and staff who get pulled into problems |
| **Brand idea** | A serious tool that does not take itself too seriously |
| **Feeling** | Dependable, precise and calm under pressure, with a wink |
| **Design language** | Industrial design that is classic, minimal and led by material. Inspired by Teenage Engineering's playful rigour and Bang & Olufsen's restraint |
| **Materials** | Stainless steel and aluminium, in honest finishes |
| **Constraints** | Must work as a Slack avatar, on a 2.8-inch screen, etched into steel and on a desk; tough enough for a pocket and a toolbox; accessible contrast everywhere |
| **Deliverables** | These guidelines, a palette, type, voice, a logo direction, finish specs for the device and rules for the interface |

**The tension to hold.** Premium, durable and playful pull against each other. The rule that settles it: the silhouette and materials are calm and precise, and the playfulness lives in details, words and sounds, never in the shape.

**Three tests every design must pass**

1. **Desk test:** would someone leave it on their desk instead of in a drawer?
2. **Squint test:** is it recognisable as Steward when blurred to a silhouette?
3. **Crisis test:** can someone read the critical screen at a glance, with one hand, under stress?

## Brand essence

Steward is the dependable tool in the office drawer that happens to be clever: precise in a crisis, light-hearted the rest of the time.

**Positioning line:** *The office manager's pocket tool.*

**Tagline options** (pick one after testing)

- Fix it. Log it. Move on.
- Sees it. Fixes it. Remembers it.
- The tool your office forgot it needed.

**Personality.** Steward is a good engineer: calm, exact, a little dry, and happy to show its working.

| We are | We are not |
| --- | --- |
| Precise, calm, dependable | Cold, clinical, corporate |
| Premium in material, modest in manner | Luxury for show, or flashy tech |
| Playful in the small things | A mascot, or a cartoon assistant |
| Durable and repairable | Disposable gadget design |
| Honest about what we know and don't | Magic, or "AI-powered" hype |
| Minimal | Bare. Every part earns its place |

**Brand promise:** Steward does the small things so you can do the big ones, and it tells you plainly what it did.

## Design principles

Six rules decide the hard calls; when two conflict, the lower-numbered one wins.

1. **Tool first, gadget second.** If a detail does not help someone fix something, remove it. It should feel like a tool someone has owned for ten years.
2. **Honest materials.** Steel looks like steel and aluminium like aluminium. No fake textures, no painted-on metal, no plastic dressed up as metal.
3. **Show the function.** Every control is labelled, etched or obvious. Nothing is hidden behind a gesture that cannot be learnt in one try.
4. **Calm by default, loud only when it matters.** The product is quiet and neutral until something is wrong, and then it is clear. Colour and sound are saved for meaning.
5. **Playful in the details.** A wink in the microcopy, a satisfying click, a surprising colour on a key. Never in the silhouette, and never during a critical issue.
6. **Built to be repaired.** Visible fasteners, replaceable battery and tools, parts that can be serviced. Durable is part of premium.

## Materials and finish

The device is aluminium for the body and stainless steel for everything you touch to work, with polish kept for a few small jewels. Grades and processes below are design intent, to be confirmed with a manufacturer.

| Part | Material | Finish | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Body shell** | Aluminium, machined or extruded | Fine bead-blast, anodised | Light, cool in the hand, takes colour, wears well |
| **Tools (all six)** | Stainless steel | Brushed along the length, one laser-etched icon each | Hard-wearing and corrosion-resistant; the etch names the job |
| **Tool cover plate** | Smoked clear window | Gloss | Lets you see the tools folded, like a watch caseback |
| **Scroll wheel and keys** | Aluminium or stainless | Machined knurl; one anodised accent colour on the push-to-talk key | Tactile, instantly findable by thumb |
| **Camera barrel** | Stainless steel with dark glass lenses | Brushed barrel, polished lens ring | The "eyes" are the one place polish earns attention |
| **Screen** | Chemically strengthened glass | Oleophobic coating | Scratch- and fingerprint-resistant |
| **Fasteners** | Stainless Torx | Visible, four on the back | Honest and serviceable; a feature, not a flaw |
| **Pivot pins** | Stainless steel | Polished heads | Signals the knife heritage |
| **Seals and feet** | Silicone | Graphite or accent | Absorbs knocks, keeps it from sliding off a desk |

**Finish rules**

1. Two finishes per face at most: one matt, one brushed. Polish is jewellery, used only on pins and the lens ring.
2. Edges are slightly broken (a small chamfer or radius) so they catch light and never feel sharp.
3. Engraving, not printing, for anything permanent: icons, labels, serial number.

**Colourways**

- **Natural:** clear-anodised aluminium that reads as raw metal. The hero finish.
- **Graphite:** dark anodised, for offices that want it quiet.
- **Signal:** one playful limited colour on the body, such as yellow or green, with the same steel tools.

**Durability intent (targets to set with the manufacturer):** survives desk-height drops, pocket and toolbox carry without visible wear, and can be wiped clean with office cleaner.

## Industrial design language

A calm slab with one big radius, a strict grid and a few tactile controls, where each surface does one job. We borrow principles from our references, not their looks.

**Inspiration without imitation**

| Reference | What we take | What we leave |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Teenage Engineering** | Playful rigour: a visible grid, labelled functions, tactile controls, one bright accent, mono labels | Their signature colours and toy-like graphics |
| **Bang & Olufsen** | Material honesty, sculptural calm, machined aluminium, finish that rewards touching | Luxury signalling and hi-fi cliches |
| **Swiss Army knife** | Tools stacked on shared pivots, everything folds away, built to last decades | Red plastic scales and feature clutter |
| **Rabbit R1** | The card-stack screen, side wheel and push-to-talk as a quick, physical way to talk to AI | The all-orange, gadget look |

**Form rules**

1. **One outer radius** for the body (6.5 mm in the concept model). Inner details derive from it; no mixed small radii.
2. **A 4 mm grid** governs the position of every control, label and fastener.
3. **The front face is the screen** plus a mic and speaker slot row and one LED. Nothing else.
4. **All controls live on the edges**, under the thumb: a scroll wheel and a push-to-talk key, with USB-C on the opposite edge.
5. **Symmetry is broken once**, by the wheel and key on the right. That asymmetry is the device's personality.
6. **The back is the tools.** A flat spine with a smoked window, four visible Torx screws and two polished pivot pins.

**Playful details** (small, repeatable, never in the silhouette)

- A wheel with real detents and a soft mechanical click.
- A camera barrel that ratchets round, and "looks up" when it is listening.
- A consistent line-icon style for the etched tools, with one deliberately cheeky icon, such as the jam grabber's frowning sheet of paper.
- An engraved serial that reads like a name: *No. 0042, Steward*.
- A tool-roll style box that unfolds to a one-line hello.

## Colour

The palette is the metals themselves, with Steward Green for the brand and Signal Yellow for the moments that matter to a voice or a key.

&#91;embedded content: palette · 11 colours in 3 groups, hex values and roles\]

**Rules**

1. Most of any screen or surface is a material neutral. Colour is the exception.
2. **Steward Green** is the brand and the main action. **Signal Yellow** is reserved for voice, live states and the push-to-talk key.
3. The four severity colours mean severity and nothing else; never use them as decoration.
4. Signal Yellow is a fill, never text or a thin line. On Paper or Aluminium it only reaches 1.3 : 1, so give it a steel border or set it on Graphite.
5. In dark mode, surfaces use Graphite and Ink, the green lifts to a lighter tint (#5CC0A4) and the severity colours lighten to keep contrast.

**Contrast checked** (WCAG relative luminance, normal text needs at least 4.5:1)

| Pair | Ratio | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ink on Paper | 15.5 : 1 | Body text |
| Ink on Aluminium | 13.4 : 1 | Text on cards |
| White on Steward Green | 6.0 : 1 | Primary buttons |
| Steward Green on Paper | 5.5 : 1 | Links and active states |
| Ink on Signal Yellow | 11.6 : 1 | Text on the yellow key or chip |
| White on Alert Red | 6.0 : 1 | Critical badge |
| White on Focus Orange | 4.6 : 1 | High badge |
| White on Circuit Blue | 6.5 : 1 | Normal badge |
| White on Quiet Grey | 5.1 : 1 | Low badge |

Hex values are design proposals; the anodising and print colours need matching to physical samples.

## Typography

Two families do everything: a clean grotesque for reading and a monospace for anything that looks etched or measured.

- **Geist Sans** for headings, body and the wordmark. It is open source (SIL Open Font License) and already used in the prototype.
- **Geist Mono** for labels, ticket IDs, specs and anything engraved. Mono is the nod to Teenage Engineering's labelled grids.
- Fallback: the system UI font. Never a serif, and never a third family.

| Role | Size | Weight | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Display | 40 | 600 | Hero lines, device boot screen |
| Title | 28 | 600 | Page titles |
| Heading | 20 | 600 | Section titles, card titles |
| Body | 16 | 400 | Reading text |
| Small | 14 | 400 | Secondary text, table cells |
| Label | 11, mono, caps | 500 | Etched-style labels, ticket IDs, units |

**Rules**

1. Two weights only, 400 and 600.
2. Sentence case everywhere. All caps is only for mono labels, with +8% letter-spacing.
3. Numbers use tabular figures so columns line up like a spec sheet.
4. On the device screen, nothing under 16 px equivalent, and the critical state uses the Display size.

## Voice and tone

Steward sounds like a good engineer on your side: short sentences, plain words, a dry wit that switches off as the stakes go up.

**The playfulness dial.** The more serious the issue, the less Steward jokes. The same dial sets the spoken voice.

| Severity | Tone | Spoken voice | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Critical** | Calm, plain, no humour | Calm and steady | "Fuse tripped, circuit 7. Unplug everything in the kitchen, then find switch 7." |
| **High** | Focused, no jokes | Clear and focused | "The meeting-room access point is offline. Unplug port 3 for ten seconds." |
| **Normal** | Friendly, light dry wit | Friendly | "The grinder's jammed. Tip out a third of the beans and give the burr a brush." |
| **Low** | Playful | Light and casual | "The Monstera is thirsty. Give it a drink, and I'll book the plant people." |

**Writing rules**

1. **Say what you did, then what is next.** "I merged two reports into STW-118. Reset switch 7 next."
2. **Name the thing.** "The cyan toner", not "a consumable".
3. **Be honest about confidence.** "I'm 74% sure it's the grinder. Want to check the hopper?"
4. **Own mistakes briefly.** "That was the wrong supplier. Fixed, and I've told them."
5. **No hype.** Never "AI-powered", "seamless" or "supercharge". No exclamation marks in any critical or high message.
6. **Wit is dry, never cute.** No emoji in guidance, no baby talk, no pretending to be human.

| Do say | Don't say |
| --- | --- |
| "Fixed. I told #office-help." | "Awesome news! Your issue has been resolved!" |
| "Nothing here. Nice." | "No tasks found." |
| "Escalated to Spark & Co, with the photo and what we tried." | "We have notified the relevant stakeholder." |

## Interface, motion and sound

The interface should feel like the device: flat, precise, tactile and quiet until something needs you.

**Interface**

1. **Flat and material.** Aluminium cards on a Paper background, 1 px steel borders, one corner radius per element family. No gradients, no drop shadows.
2. **Photos lead.** A ticket starts with the photo, because that is how the problem arrived.
3. **One primary action per screen**, in Steward Green. The rest are quiet outline buttons.
4. **Mono for the measured stuff:** ticket IDs, phone numbers, costs, units.
5. **Icons** are 1.8 px line icons with round caps, one consistent set.
6. **Severity is a dot and a label, never colour alone.** Critical also gets the Display type size on the device.
7. **Device screen:** a black card stack in the style of a physical index, one thumb-sized card per job, the urgent one on top.

**Motion**

- Mechanical, not bouncy: 150 to 250 ms, ease-out, things slide and click into place.
- The one flourish is a detent: a small, quick settle when a card or wheel position locks.
- Respect reduced-motion settings everywhere; nothing important depends on animation.

**Sound** (a direction to prototype, not a finished set)

| Moment | Sound |
| --- | --- |
| Wheel turn | A soft mechanical detent click |
| Voice listening | A short two-tone chirp, rising |
| Task fixed | A low, satisfying clunk, like a latch closing |
| Critical alert | One calm low tone, no siren, then the calm voice |
| Boot | A tiny click-and-chirp, under a second |

Sound is always optional, and every spoken message also appears as text.

## Logo and mark

Three directions are drawn below; the pivot mark is the recommendation because it is the one idea that comes from the product itself.

&#91;embedded content: logo directions · 3 options, pivot recommended\]

These are working sketches to choose a direction from, not finished artwork; the final mark needs drawing and testing at small sizes.

**Why the pivot.** It is the pin every folding tool turns on, so it ties the brand to the Swiss Army heritage and the physical device. It also reads as a lens, which fits the camera. It etches cleanly into steel in one colour.

**Rules for the chosen mark**

1. **Wordmark:** lowercase "steward" in Geist Sans, weight 600, with the pivot mark beside or above it.
2. **Clear space** equals the diameter of the inner pin on every side.
3. **Minimum size:** 16 px on screen and 6 mm etched.
4. **Colour:** Ink on Paper or Aluminium; white on Graphite or Steward Green. One-colour version (ring and slot only) for etching and stamping.
5. **Never** stretch, outline, add gradients or shadows, or place the mark on a busy photo.

## Do and don't, and references

When a design choice is unclear, check it against this table first.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Show real metal finishes, lit from one side | Fake brushed-metal textures or chrome gradients |
| Use the grid and one radius | Mix many corner radii or free-float elements |
| Keep the front clean: screen and nothing else | Add decorative lines, badges or logos on the front |
| Spend playfulness on details, words and sound | Make the silhouette cute or toy-like |
| Reserve colour for meaning | Colour things just to liven them up |
| Etch labels and icons into the metal | Print them on in a way that wears off |
| Make repair visible and easy | Hide fasteners behind glue or stickers |
| Keep critical messages plain and calm | Use humour, hype or alarm styling when it is serious |

**References to study** (for principles, not to copy)

- **Teenage Engineering:** the OP-1 and the Pocket Operator range, for labelled grids, tactile controls and the single accent colour.
- **Bang & Olufsen:** the Beosound A1 and Beolit, for machined aluminium, quiet proportions and finish.
- **Victorinox and Leatherman:** the Swiss Army knife and the Wave, for stacked tools and decades-long durability.
- **Braun:** the T3 pocket radio, for calm layouts and one thing per surface.
- **Rabbit R1:** the card-stack screen, side wheel and push-to-talk, as a model for talking to AI with your thumb.

Build a moodboard in three groups, **metal and finish**, **controls and labels**, and **small jokes**, and keep it next to the 3D model while designing.
